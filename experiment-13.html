EXPERIMENT 13 · REGISTERED 2026-11-27 · RUNNING

Six retail signals, relaunched on the S&P 500

Six techniques came back inconclusive on our 40-name universe: the tests could rule out large edges, not small ones. This is the same six rules, unchanged, on 503 names, with the sample size worked out in advance from the effect we are looking for. This page is the design, frozen before the first signal.

STATUS · RUNNING · NO RESULT YET
One verdict per arm, computed once, when the sample is big enough to answer.

Until each arm reaches its floors (150 signal sessions, 1,000 trades, 100 companies) we publish counts only: no running average, no interval, no verdict language. At the rates seen on 40 names, scaled to 503, we expect the floors in about seven months, around July 2027. The counts update daily in the live experiments report.

Why a relaunch

Experiments 02, 03 and 06–09 all ended inconclusive. That label was honest, but on its own it says less than it should. Experiment 03 is typical: its interval ruled out a same-day edge bigger than about ±0.3 points per trade, and the test had only about a 10% chance of catching a 0.10-point edge (the full read). The question was right; the sample could not answer it.

The first run used 40 liquid large caps on purpose: a 0.10% round trip meant costs could not explain a null, a fixed list could not drift, and a short list kept the daily download small on the free data feed this project runs on. What it lacked was a sample sized for the effect we cared about, and no registration named that effect. Since 2026-11-25 every registration must (HYPOTHESES.md). This is the first one built that way.

This experiment is about method first. Whatever it finds, it is built to be able to say so.

What the first run taught us about sample size

Signals that fire on the same day move together. In the six locked 40-name samples, the same-day correlation of the 5-session excess was about 0.35, so ten trades on one day carry the evidence of roughly 2.4 independent ones. Trading sessions, not names, are the binding unit. A bigger universe fills every session and lets us take more signals a day. It cannot replace time, which is why the floors are set in sessions.

The design, frozen

Arms
13A MACD bullish crossover · 13B 2-period RSI · 13C Supertrend flip · 13D moving-average pullback · 13E Bollinger reversion · 13F NR7 contraction. Each uses its registered entry rule, unchanged.
Universe
503 S&P 500 share classes across all 11 sectors, frozen from a public constituent list on 2026-11-26 (universes/sp500-2026-11-26.csv, source and checksum in the registration). Large caps only, so the 0.10% cost stays credible. Adding or removing a name voids the test.
Entry and exit
Signals evaluated after the close, logged before the next open; at most 10 per arm per day, the registered tie-break first. Entry at the next open. Primary exit: the fifth session’s close. The same-session result is reported as secondary and cannot rescue a fail.
Control
The equal-weight return of the rest of the universe over the identical window, leaving the traded name out.
Costs
0.10% round trip, charged to the trade only, so the excess is the edge over simply holding the universe.
Window
Only signals dated strictly after 2026-11-27 count.

The sample size, worked out first

Smallest effect we care about: ±0.75 percentage points per 5-session trade.
Error rates: two-sided α = 0.05/6, so a single arm can survive the family correction, and 80% power.
Inputs from the first run: a 5-session standard deviation of about 4.0 points and a same-day correlation of about 0.35.
Result: about 344 independent trades’ worth of evidence, or about 143 sessions at 10 signals a day. Floor: 150 sessions, plus at least 1,000 trades and 100 companies. The arithmetic is in planned_sessions() in experiment_harness.py, pinned by a test.

Amended 2026-11-26, before any Experiment 13 signal existed: the registration set ±0.50 points and a 330-session floor, about 16 months. We moved to ±0.75 and 150 sessions to get a read in about seven months. The cost: an “equivalent to zero” verdict now rules out only edges of 0.75 points or more, and a smaller real edge would more likely come back underpowered. Nothing had been observed when the change was made; the amendment is in HYPOTHESES.md.

What it can conclude

Four outcomes, fixed now, with intervals from standard errors clustered by both company and day:

Clears the bar: the 95% interval is above zero, the mean and median are both positive, and the direction held for three weekly snapshots.
Significantly negative: the 95% interval is below zero.
Equivalent to zero: the 90% interval sits inside ±0.75 points, so any edge is smaller than the one we care about. This is the result the first run could not give.
Underpowered: none of the above.

The six arms are one family: a pass also has to survive a Benjamini–Hochberg correction across all six. They share a universe and many days, so they are correlated tests, not six independent replications.

What we expect

Our registered expectation is that every arm comes back equivalent to zero. These rules are published, taught and widely followed, and none of them showed anything on 40 names. If one clears the bar, that would be genuinely surprising, and we will say so, with the same scope limits as every verdict here.

An early look backward: 2005–2025 (exploratory, not the verdict)

Over 21 years of past prices, none of the six rules beat simply holding the rest of the S&P 500 over the same five sessions. Four did reliably worse, by about 0.1 to 0.2 points per trade: roughly the 0.10% cost plus a little. The other two were indistinguishable from the control. Every interval sits well inside ±0.75 points.

What this backtest can’t tell you

Missing companies. The point-in-time list only works for names the free data feed still carries. It covered 53% of member-days in 2005, rising to 97% by 2025. Most of the gap is companies that were acquired or failed, so the early years lean toward survivors. Today’s 503 lean on survivors entirely. The two lists agree closely, which is reassuring but not proof.
No dividends, in the trade or the control, so they mostly cancel. Data errors in a free feed are possible. The past need not hold: a 21-year average says little about 2027.

Every trade is public: summary, by year, coverage, all trades, and the script, backtest_exp13.py, whose selftest checks each rule bar for bar against the forward harness.

How you’ll be able to check it

Every signal is written to experiments/EXP13*-signals.csv before its entry session, and graded outcomes alongside it, both public and append-only. Verdicts are appended once to experiments/EXP13-verdicts.csv and the family correction to experiments/EXP13-fdr.csv. The runs happen in the project’s daily public GitHub workflow.

What this experiment is not

It is not a recommendation to trade this or anything else, and nothing here is advice. It is not a claim about any author, platform or educator. It does not reopen any earlier verdict. And whichever way it lands, it describes these rules, on this list, over this window.
